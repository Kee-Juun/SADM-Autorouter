# Out of Spec: The Archivist Trials

## Level One Narrative Outline - The Archivist Trials

**Status:** Production narrative draft 0.1  
**Parent canon:** `docs/OUT_OF_SPEC_LORE_BIBLE.md`  
**Scope:** Opening through the Level Two handoff  
**Spoiler level:** Internal development document. Do not expose to players.

---

## 1. Level Promise

Level One should feel like a strange first day at a magical workplace where the building is haunted, the supervisor is funny, and the filing problem is probably manageable.

By the end, the player should understand that:

- The Archive changes reality rather than merely recording it.
- Memory, Mercy, and Order are necessary but dangerous when isolated.
- The Bellglass Fire was deliberate.
- Docket VII-13 was concealed through recognized authority.
- Unresolved petitions were deliberately transferred below the public Archive.
- The Pending is intelligent and has been trapped for a very long time.
- Mara cares about the Last Clerk for reasons neither fully understands.

The player must not yet learn that:

- The Pending is a collective of human victims.
- Mara and the Last Clerk share an original soul.
- Orren Vale survives.
- Lysa Marr survives through temporal deferral.
- Elian Vey remains conscious inside the Chronometer.
- Repeated reconstruction caused the Last Clerk's deeper amnesia.

The final emotional note is not triumph. It is curiosity disturbed by responsibility.

## 2. Tone Curve

| Phase | Surface Feeling | Hidden Pressure | Player Relationship Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Awakening | Warm, odd, lightly comic | The Hall recognizes the Clerk | Trust Mara enough to continue |
| First hall | Adventure and discovery | The system's principles can harm people | Build competence and curiosity |
| First return | Friendly relief | Mara knows his habits too well | Establish chemistry and unease |
| Second hall | Confidence | Human choices caused the damage | Complicate simple blame |
| Second return | Familiarity | Mara fears what she is noticing | Make the relationship personally valuable |
| Third hall | Investigation and danger | Docket VII-13 was buried deliberately | Convert mystery into crime |
| Seal puzzles | Mastery and ritual | The halls were preparing a witness | Make mechanics carry meaning |
| Vault | Gothic spectacle | The monster knows the institution intimately | Confront what delay creates |
| Aftermath | Earned victory | The defeated entity leaves human names behind | Open Level Two through moral obligation |

Humor remains present throughout, but its job changes. It begins as charm, becomes friendship, and finally becomes courage under pressure.

---

## 3. Narrative Architecture

Level One uses a **hub-and-testimony** structure.

1. The Hall of Pending Things introduces Mara, the vault, and the three failed seals.
2. Memory, Mercy, and Order may be completed in any order.
3. Returning to the hub after each first-time hall completion triggers a short relationship interlude.
4. Each relic unlocks its matching main-hall seal puzzle.
5. Each solved seal produces one key fragment and one piece of historical testimony.
6. The assembled key opens the Vault of the Unclosed.
7. Defeating The Pending yields the Codex of the Unclosed and points toward the Court of Erased Names.

The hall order is non-linear. The emotional spine is not. Interludes are keyed to the number of completed trials rather than a prescribed room sequence.

## 4. Character Knowledge Ledger

### 4.1 The Last Clerk

**Knows at opening:** His title, basic language, procedural instincts, and how to use his lantern.  
**Does not know:** His personal history, why the Hall accepts him, why Mara feels familiar, or how long he was absent.  
**Incorrect belief:** His memory loss is probably temporary and solving the Archive will restore it cleanly.  
**Level One movement:** From practical detachment to choosing responsibility for people he has never met.

### 4.2 Mara

**Knows at opening:** She performed a forbidden Writ, something escaped, the seals are failing, and the Last Clerk resembles an answer she chose not to remember.  
**Suspects:** He may be connected to her missing Motion.  
**Does not know:** Whether he is the survivor, whether he is stable, or whether recognition would merge or kill them.  
**Incorrect belief:** She can safely investigate him without emotionally involving either of them.  
**Level One movement:** From guarded professional warmth to frightened affection and a decision to seek evidence before speaking.

### 4.3 The Pending

**Knows:** The Archive's crimes, fragments of Mara's Writ, and that the Last Clerk is archivally incomplete.  
**Wants:** The vault opened and an outcome forced.  
**Restriction:** It cannot explain the complete truth coherently because its voices disagree and the ward-seals redact direct testimony.  
**Level One movement:** From distant pressure to theatrical antagonist to a defeated shell that leaves evidence of many speakers.

### 4.4 The Archive

**Knows:** Structural truth but cannot interpret justice.  
**Wants:** A consistent filing.  
**Behavior:** Accepts the Last Clerk where ordinary identity checks would fail, creating the first fair clues about his nature.

## 5. Prologue: The Clerk Who Woke Late

### 5.1 Opening Image

Black screen. A bell rings once. Before its echo ends, a second bell begins backward. Loose pages rise from the floor and return to torn ledgers. A thin cyan line traces the silhouette of the Last Clerk before his body resolves on the Hall floor.

Mara is already present, seated beside a dead lamp rather than waiting in a dramatic hero pose. She has been watching him breathe.

### 5.2 First Conversation

The scene must establish personality before exposition.

**MARA:** You are awake. Excellent. The Archive has been screaming alphabetically, which is somehow worse than ordinary screaming.

**LAST CLERK:** How long was I asleep?

**MARA:** Long enough to miss orientation. Fortunate. It was mostly forms and one preventable haunting.

**LAST CLERK:** Who are you?

Mara pauses. His tone and head tilt are familiar.

**MARA:** Mara Venn. Senior Clerk. Local prisoner. Occasional medical professional when you make poor decisions.

**LAST CLERK:** And me?

**MARA:** The Archive calls you its Last Clerk.

**LAST CLERK:** That sounds less like a title and more like an accusation.

**MARA:** Good. Your judgment survived.

The player receives three response choices. These shape flavor and later callbacks but do not change canon:

1. **"Tell me what is broken."** Practical response.
2. **"Start with why I cannot remember my name."** Vulnerable response.
3. **"You watched me wake up and led with the screaming building?"** Humorous response.

Mara answers honestly but incompletely. She says the ward-seals failed, the vault is pressing against them, and the three side halls contain the means to restore control. If asked about his identity, she states that his personal record is absent, not merely damaged. She does not pretend this is normal.

### 5.3 First Recognition Beat

When the Last Clerk picks up his lantern, he adjusts its rear clasp without looking. Mara quietly says, "You always hated that catch," then stops.

**LAST CLERK:** Always?

**MARA:** Every clerk hates that catch. It was a statistical always.

This is the first shared-identity clue. It must be noticeable but dismissible.

### 5.4 Tutorial Objective

The player walks around the Hall, inspects Mara, the three dormant seals, and the vault lock. The UI introduces interaction and the collapsible relic belt through objects, not floating developer instructions.

The vault emits one line when approached:

**THE VAULT:** REQUEST REMAINS PENDING.

Mara hears it but pretends to inspect her sleeve.

## 6. The Three Hall Modules

The halls may be completed in any order. Each module contains:

1. A threshold scene.
2. A mechanical lesson.
3. Environmental testimony.
4. A danger or failure state.
5. A relic reward.
6. A changed Mara conversation on return.

### 6.1 Hall of Memory: What Is Remembered

#### Threshold

The Hall reacts to the Last Clerk before he touches anything. The Past exhales dust, the Present flickers once, and the Future briefly shows him standing in a position he has not reached.

**LAST CLERK:** Friendly room.

**MARA, through the lantern:** It likes you.

**LAST CLERK:** That was your reassuring voice?

**MARA:** No. You will recognize that one by the lying.

#### Play Structure

- Activate Past, Present, or Future in any order.
- Search highlighted room objects for the matching hourglass.
- Past reverses movement.
- Present accelerates movement.
- Future slows and corrupts movement.
- Return each hourglass to its Archive before time closes.
- The player cannot leave until all three are restored.

#### Narrative Function of the Searches

Search messages should do more than joke. Each temporal state tells a different kind of truth:

- **Past:** What an object witnessed and what official history omitted.
- **Present:** What the object is doing now, including institutional absurdity.
- **Future:** A possible consequence, never guaranteed prophecy.

Every searchable object therefore gives the player three layers of the same world. Repeated inspection after the first search receives a short character joke rather than another lore dump.

#### The Three Archive Lessons

**Past:** Intention is not control. The player must adapt when familiar inputs produce reversed consequences.

**Present:** Speed is not clarity. The player can move quickly but has less time to observe.

**Future:** Possibility is not destiny. Glitching echoes show alternatives rather than instructions.

#### Completion

Restoring all three causes the center floor inscription to read:

> A record without sequence can be made to confess anything.

The Recall Lens forms from the light above the Present Archive. When the Last Clerk touches it, a man's indistinct voice says, "At last," one second before the Last Clerk hears it.

Mara claims she heard only static. She is telling the truth.

#### What This Foreshadows

- Vey persists inside temporal mechanisms.
- The Last Clerk has counterfactual echoes.
- Recorded sequence matters as much as recorded content.
- Mara cannot observe everything the Last Clerk experiences.

### 6.2 Hall of Mercy: Who Pays

#### Threshold

The Red Tape Wraith is binding a translucent petitioner to the central dais. The petitioner has no detailed face yet; Level One must not fully reveal that The Pending contains people.

The Wraith recites procedural objections while tightening crimson bands.

**WRAITH:** Mercy request lacks proof of deserving mercy.

**LAST CLERK:** Convenient standard.

**WRAITH:** Convenience is admissible.

The fight triggers automatically when the Wraith notices him.

#### Combat Meaning

The Wraith weaponizes conditions, delays, and binding clauses. The player's lesson is that a procedure can appear neutral while being designed to prevent relief.

The spectral petitioner disappears when the Wraith falls. The player cannot know whether they were freed, erased, or merely moved.

#### Environmental Testimony

After combat, three appeal tablets become readable:

1. A pardon granted to a noble whose punishment later appeared in a servant's record.
2. A village spared from flood while an unmapped settlement vanished downstream.
3. A child's fever removed from history while the attending physician remembered a patient no one else did.

These establish transferred consequence without explaining Project Continuance.

#### Completion

The Appeal Tonic condenses in a vessel that is warm despite the cold room.

Inscription:

> Mercy that hides its cost has only changed the name of cruelty.

The Last Clerk asks Mara whether every mercy demands a victim. Mara answers:

**MARA:** No. But every change has a consequence. Anyone claiming otherwise has already chosen who will pay and decided not to introduce you.

#### What This Foreshadows

- Continuance transfers costs.
- Mara's resurrection appeal required a price.
- Healing and revival are not free, even when the immediate UI cost appears to be gold.

### 6.3 Lower Registry: Who Authorized the Lie

#### Threshold

The room is orderly enough to feel staged. Three investigative stations contain fragments of Docket VII-13. The central chest breathes almost imperceptibly.

The Last Clerk may inspect the chest early.

**CENTRAL CHEST:** NO CONTRADICTIONS FOUND.

**LAST CLERK:** You said that before I asked.

**CENTRAL CHEST:** EFFICIENCY COMMENDED.

#### Investigation Structure

- Identity identifies Elian Vey.
- Sequence restores the order of discovery, copy, authorization, and fire.
- Authority identifies the Black Sun seal of Final Disposition.
- Each success places one finding into the Master Docket.
- The central chest becomes more visibly unstable as contradictions return.

Hints must teach reasoning rather than provide answers. Wrong answers produce a new clue or eliminate one interpretation. Failure never discards evidence already understood.

#### Required Wording About Vale

Level One evidence proves:

- Vale's office possessed the authority used for the transfer.
- His Black Sun seal appears on the order.
- The order preceded the Bellglass Fire.
- The official chronology concealed that sequence.

Level One must not claim that Vale personally ordered the fire or understood every consequence. The restored record should say:

> The Black Sun authority carried Docket VII-13 below the public Archive. Orren Vale's seal made concealment lawful. The surviving record does not yet say whose command lit the Bellglass Fire.

This preserves guilt without pretending the investigation is finished.

#### Mimic Reveal

Once all findings are restored, the central chest attempts to classify them as mutually impossible. Its body opens into the Misfiled Mimic.

**MIMIC:** Truth is the version of a record that survives filing.

**LAST CLERK:** Then filing has mistaken survival for innocence.

The mimic battle tests Identity, Sequence, and Authority through its changing sigils.

#### Completion

The Reset Compass points not north but toward the most recent contradiction.

Inscription:

> Order can preserve truth. It can also preserve the shape of a lie.

#### What This Foreshadows

- Vale's genuine responsibility.
- Later evidence may complicate but never erase that responsibility.
- The Archive verifies consistency rather than justice.
- The Bellglass Fire was an act of institutional concealment.

## 7. Hub Interludes

These trigger after the player returns from each newly completed hall. Their content adapts to the hall just completed, but their emotional function follows completion count.

### 7.1 After the First Hall: Competence

Mara is relieved and tries to disguise it as quality control.

**MARA:** You survived.

**LAST CLERK:** You sound surprised.

**MARA:** I sound accurate. Surprise is what accuracy wears when it has been worried.

She inspects the relic in his hand and stands closer than necessary. Both notice; neither comments directly.

Player response:

1. **"Were you worried about me?"**
2. **"Tell me what this relic does."**
3. **"I knew you would miss me."**

Mara's answer changes tone but not relationship state. The game records whether the player tends toward vulnerability, practicality, or humor for later callbacks.

### 7.2 After the Second Hall: Familiarity

Mara has prepared tea in two cups despite claiming she does not drink while on duty.

The Last Clerk takes the cup she intended for herself without asking. It is exactly how he likes it. Mara watches him drink.

**MARA:** Of course.

**LAST CLERK:** Bad tea?

**MARA:** Disturbingly correct tea.

This scene should be quiet. The player can ask about Mara's life before the fire. She gives one ordinary memory unrelated to the conspiracy: stealing sugared pears from a records banquet with Lysa Marr. This makes Lysa a person before she becomes a mystery.

### 7.3 After the Third Hall: Unease

The three relics resonate. Mara briefly experiences pain in the same place where the Last Clerk was most recently injured.

He notices her hand move to the wound.

**LAST CLERK:** You felt that.

**MARA:** The seals are connected.

**LAST CLERK:** That was not what I said.

Mara does not lie directly. She says she does not yet understand it and asks for time to verify what she suspects. This establishes a promise she will later strain but has not yet broken.

## 8. The Three Seal Puzzles

The hall relics are prerequisites because experience must precede authority. Finding an object is not enough; the player must demonstrate the lesson it carries.

### 8.1 Memory Seal: The Palimpsest Chronometer

**Relic:** Recall Lens  
**Test:** Inspect concealed testimonies, remember their content, and arrange them in causal sequence.  
**Meaning:** Memory without sequence can be manipulated.

The five scrolls describe a keeper discovering missing petitions, a copy being made, an order being sealed, the fire beginning, and the evidence falling silent. Symbols replace numeric labels so the player reasons from testimony rather than assuming a convenient order.

On failure, the Chronometer shows a plausible but false history and stamps it **CONSISTENT**. This is the lesson: coherence alone does not create truth.

### 8.2 Mercy Seal: The Weighing of Exceptions

**Relic:** Appeal Tonic  
**Test:** Balance law and mercy while accounting for transferred cost.  
**Meaning:** Compassion is incomplete when its price is hidden.

The puzzle should show who bears each weight rather than using abstract numbers alone. A technically balanced solution can still be morally wrong if all harm is assigned to someone absent from the hearing.

The successful solution distributes unavoidable cost among informed parties and leaves one weight unassigned as acknowledged uncertainty. Perfect certainty is not required for responsible mercy.

### 8.3 Order Seal: The Coupled Registry

**Relic:** Reset Compass  
**Test:** Restore linked indices whose movements affect one another.  
**Meaning:** No classification exists in isolation.

Each ring represents Identity, Sequence, Authority, and Consequence. Moving one changes adjacent rings. The player must stop treating each fact as an independent switch.

Failure produces a neat but impossible filing. Success leaves minor visual imperfections, showing that truthful order need not look perfectly symmetrical.

### 8.4 Fragment Testimonies

Each solved seal releases one sentence:

- **Memory Fragment:** "Seven petitions vanished from ink but remained in wax."
- **Mercy Fragment:** "Their unanswered costs were assigned to no living witness."
- **Order Fragment:** "A Black Sun transfer carried them below public review."

When fused, the statements form the key's inner inscription:

> WHAT WAS UNANSWERED WAS NOT GONE.

## 9. Before the Vault

### 9.1 Key Fusion

The player manually combines the fragments in the relic belt. Each connection briefly plays one testimony over another. The completed key should look like a hybrid of all three jurisdictions, not a generic golden key.

### 9.2 Mara's Last Level-One Conversation

This conversation is mandatory before the lock accepts the key.

**MARA:** Once that door opens, I cannot follow you.

**LAST CLERK:** You could have led with that several halls ago.

**MARA:** You would have gone anyway.

**LAST CLERK:** Probably.

**MARA:** I know.

The word lands too personally.

The player may ask why she is bound to the Hall. Mara says the fire wrote her into its foundations. This is emotionally true but metaphysically incomplete.

Before the player leaves, Mara adjusts the Last Clerk's lantern clasp. Her hands perform the motion exactly as his did during the opening.

**MARA:** Come back with the same number of souls you entered with.

**LAST CLERK:** Is that a common workplace concern?

**MARA:** It is now.

### 9.3 Lock Interaction

The key must be dragged into the illuminated keyhole and deliberately turned. The door does not open automatically because entering the Vault must be an authored choice, not an accidental proximity trigger.

## 10. Vault of the Unclosed

### 10.1 Entrance

The Vault is initially quiet. Shadow Clerks kneel around the distant throne. The Pending appears regal and controlled, not immediately monstrous.

It refers to the Last Clerk as **Unentered Motion**. The Clerk does not understand the phrase. Mara cannot hear it clearly through the ward boundary.

### 10.2 The Pending's Argument

The Pending should make one valid argument before the fight:

**THE PENDING:** Your Archive calls delay neutral. Delay has hunger. Delay consumes the person who waits and feeds the person who benefits from never answering.

The Last Clerk agrees that the Archive failed it, but rejects its demand to force every suspended consequence into the present at once.

**LAST CLERK:** You deserved an answer. You do not get to make everyone else become one.

This line establishes the series' position: victimhood deserves witness but does not grant unlimited authority over new victims.

### 10.3 Combat Phases

**Phase One: Administrative Control**  
The Pending commands renewable Shadow Clerks and attacks through delay, attachment, and clarification mechanics.

**Phase Two: Final Docket**  
At half health, its composure breaks. Multiple voices overlap. Human names flicker briefly across its pages, too quickly to read completely.

**Phase Three: Refusal of Closure**  
The Last Clerk must use all three relic lessons: recall an attack sequence, redirect a transferred cost, and break the authority sustaining the minions.

### 10.4 Defeat and Reconstruction Foreshadowing

If the player loses, Mara's appellate projection rescues him. She returns him to the Hall through a formal appeal.

After waking, he remembers the fight but fails to remember one harmless line from their pre-vault conversation. Mara notices. She does not explain why because she does not yet know whether the loss came from the appeal, his missing record, or their connection.

This is foreshadowing only. Level One does not remove quest progress or punish the player mechanically.

### 10.5 Victory

The Pending's shell disintegrates into smoke, embers, and torn petitions. The Shadow Clerks collapse with it. Among the papers are human names, not monster classifications. The game does not yet confirm whether these people formed The Pending, were imprisoned by it, or were merely known to it.

The Last Clerk lowers his weapon.

**LAST CLERK:** These are names. Why would it carry names?

The remaining pages answer in several voices:

**THE PENDING:** Every request belonged to someone.

This line is the emotional turn into Level Two. The Court of Erased Names later confirms that The Pending's consciousness contains the people whose possible lives were denied.

## 11. Epilogue: The First Name Missing

### 11.1 Return to Mara

Mara initially celebrates because relief escapes before professionalism can stop it. When she sees the human names in the Codex, the celebration ends.

The Last Clerk asks whether she knew. She says:

**MARA:** I knew the Vault held unresolved lives. I did not know they had learned to speak together.

This distinction is truthful and important.

### 11.2 The Codex of the Unclosed

The Codex lists petitioners absent from every public register. One entry is blank except for handwriting identical to the Last Clerk's when reflected.

The next legible heading reads:

> COURT OF ERASED NAMES

Mara asks him to wait while she investigates. He refuses, gently rather than dramatically.

**LAST CLERK:** If a missing name can still ask for help, being unlisted is not the same as being gone.

This is the first value he chooses without instruction.

### 11.3 Final Image

After the Last Clerk exits toward Level Two, Mara presses her hand to the dead lamp from the opening. It lights with the same cyan flame as his lantern.

She whispers:

**MARA:** What did I send back?

Cut away before she answers herself.

### 11.4 Optional Post-Credit Hook

At the threshold of the Court of Erased Names, a damaged adjudicator calling himself **Orin** closes a ledger before the Last Clerk can read it.

**ORIN:** You took your time.

**LAST CLERK:** Do I know you?

**ORIN:** Not if fortune has finally developed standards.

This introduces Vale as a person before the player receives the name that will condemn him.

## 12. Optional Mara Conversation Progression

Mara's hub menu should evolve with narrative state. Responses remain pooled so she never repeats like a quest terminal.

### Before Any Hall

- Practical guidance for each hall.
- Her duties before the fire.
- Why the Archive needs three jurisdictions.
- A deflection about why the Last Clerk feels familiar.

### After One Hall

- A hall-specific reaction.
- Concern disguised as assessment.
- One playful response to the player's chosen conversational tendency.

### After Two Halls

- The sugared-pear memory with Lysa.
- A question from Mara about what the Clerk wants beyond recovering his past.
- The first moment where she considers that he may have become someone new.

### After Three Halls

- Mara admits the fire damaged more than the building.
- She acknowledges feeling one of his injuries.
- She asks for time rather than demanding trust.

### After Solving All Seals

- Discussion of choice, fear, and entering the Vault.
- No generic progress summary unless requested.
- The player may reassure Mara, tease her, or ask her to be honest.

### After One Vault Defeat

- Mara asks a subtle memory-check question.
- If the Clerk does not remember, she changes the subject badly.
- The player can notice her fear but cannot yet force the full answer.

## 13. Environmental Storytelling Checklist

### Hall of Pending Things

- Scratches around the vault point outward, not inward.
- Mara's workstation contains two cups before the second interlude.
- One old clearance plate reacts to both Mara and the Last Clerk.
- The floor bears names worn away by repeated foot traffic.

### Hall of Memory

- Objects reveal different temporal truths by state.
- Vey's hooked signature appears without an explanatory label.
- Future visions remain possible outcomes, not guaranteed spoilers.

### Hall of Mercy

- Every successful appeal tablet has an obscured cost field.
- The Wraith's bindings connect to absent names beyond the room.
- The Appeal Tonic contains two fluids that never completely mix.

### Lower Registry

- The central chest's locks face inward, implying containment.
- The Black Sun seal is clear; the fire command remains incomplete.
- Iron shelves show no ordinary burn damage.

### Vault

- The throne is assembled from unopened petitions.
- Shadow Clerks carry different handwriting on otherwise identical forms.
- Human names become readable only after victory.

## 14. Dialogue Standards for Level One

1. No character uses developer terms such as trigger, quest state, phase, or checkpoint.
2. Mara never explains more than she knows.
3. The Last Clerk jokes to manage discomfort, not because every scene requires a punchline.
4. The Pending's humor is institutional and threatening.
5. Environmental text is concise enough to reward reading rather than halt play.
6. Important emotional lines receive silence before the next prompt.
7. Player choices express attitude and relationship boundaries; they do not rename or rewrite the protagonist.
8. Repeated questions draw from response pools and acknowledge changed progress.
9. No twist contradicts a previously honest scene.
10. Victims are never the targets of the joke.

## 17. Level-One Emotional Test

Before production dialogue is considered finished, a playthrough should produce five feelings in order:

1. **I like these two people.**
2. **I want to understand this place.**
3. **Something terrible happened here.**
4. **The apparent monster has a reason to be angry.**
5. **Opening the next door is now my responsibility.**

If the player only understands the plot but does not care about Mara, the Last Clerk, or the unresolved names, Level One has not succeeded.
